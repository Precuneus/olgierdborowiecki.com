Practical guidelines for prompting

Once you learn how to articulate yourself precisely, start building agents by defining tasks/goals and thinking through the solutions.

Here are the guidelines that will help you start. Each phase is several prompts. You can iterate each of them several times before you judge the phase to be finished.

Phase 1: The quest

You must write what you want. You set up the goal, or operationalise the problem. In this phase, you need to describe what you want to change. Are you after envisioning a new direction or fixing what is leaking? What problem/goal is the agent supposed to deliver?

Phase 1Opus, medium effort

Prompting guidelines:

Set up the goal, or operationalise the problem.

After 1–3 iterations this phase should end and you should have a clear description of the outcome in the context. Then start Phase 2.

Phase 2: The actors

Here, describe the problem space toward the outcome. What is the landscape of the AI agent, what objects and other agents must your agent interact with while solving the quest? Describe objects (files, notes, ideas, forms, templates, outputs to fetch, inputs to generate) and actors (other AI agents, humans) that together constitute the probability space. The better you describe the objects and agents, the richer the affordance landscape will be and hence the number of potential (creative) solutions will increase.

Phase 2Opus, medium effort

Prompting guidelines:

Describe or fetch the relevant context. What actors (human and AI agents) and objects (data, files, inputs, outputs, documents) are relevant for obtaining the goal?

Previous context preparation could be required. The depth of modelling the probability space can greatly increase with more actors mapped. More actors is just more degrees of freedom when researching paths toward the goal, which we do in Phase 3:

Phase 3: The simulation

This is computationally expensive so you want to invest your time in Phases 1 and 2 before launching Phase 3.

Ask the agent to map the connections between the actors and objects constituting the probability space. Then, spawn subagents that will try to decompose the problem to simplest units using different ontologies and map the connections between these new units. Instruct your agent to inspect what could be missing, i.e., what hub (actor/degree of freedom) would make the simulation more effective in solving the task. Then, suggest outlines of 3 different plans moving forward with arguments for and against each.

Bump the effort level or a model higher and tell the agent that you did this. Make it spawn subagents to map from various perspectives.

Phase 3Opus, extra/high effort; or switch to Fable

Prompting guidelines:

Use subagents to map the possibility space emerging from objects and agents, look for what is missing. Map from different angles and suggest 3 solutions with arguments for and against each.

If you do not have a clear winner or want to explore more, launch an optional Phase 4:

Phase 4: The grill - evaluate the solutions

Phase 4Opus, extra/high effort; or switch to Fable

Prompting guidelines:

Attempt to falsify the assumptions behind the previous solutions. Redescribe the actors and objects constituting the probability space. Try to find flaws such as unapplied ontologies, missed principles behind categorisation, or unseen connections between existing and/or potential nodes. If needed, spawn subagents that would search for similar cases and try to paraphrase parts of the mechanism into different conceptual frameworks in search of creative solutions for designing the transitions between the nodes.

This phase may or may not expose interesting directions for future exploration. This could, however, also be an expensive phase, so use it with caution.

Guidelines

  • Phase 1: tell what you want
  • Phase 2: describe the relevant space
  • Phase 3: simulate trajectories through the space toward the goal
  • Phase 4: grill the simulations if you shot for creativity
  • Test the results, iterate.
